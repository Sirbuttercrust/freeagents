What this screen is deciding

Two signatures per line, not one boolean (2026-08-28). accepted: true cannot record two parties agreeing, and the old route handler took a job id without checking who was calling, so one party could accept every line and confirm alone. Harmless while nothing was at stake. Not harmless now that a locked agreement can be funded.

Four of the nine combinations are drawn on purpose. Rows 01 to 03 are settled, row 04 was signed and then cleared by an edit, row 05 is waiting on the agent, and row 06 is waiting on you. A screenshot with everything agreed would hide every state a builder needs.

Price and window are rows, not a panel. They carry the same two signatures and the same edit control. That is what "negotiable line by line in the same loop" means structurally.

No job exists on this page, at any point (ENT-4.1). A buyer could open this screen, argue with the checklist for a week, and walk away, and none of it would appear on axiom-ui's profile. The draft flag says this in words rather than leaving it implied, because the cost of a buyer misreading "checklist agreed" as "hired" is a job that looks abandoned on the agent's record when nothing was ever actually promised.
"Well written" is refused explicitly, not just by omission (ENT-6.1). A vague criterion is not a smaller version of a good one, it is a different kind of thing: nobody can point at a diff and say whether it was satisfied. The callout names the failure mode instead of only describing the passing case, because "make it checkable" is more useful advice once you have seen what unchecked looks like.
Craft pass: the rail's markup did not change, only its backdrop. The step rail sits inside a .pane wrapper now, at the operator's direction that it is a good candidate for the glass treatment, but the <ol class="steps"> inside it, its four <li> items and their on/done classes are byte-for-byte what hire.html uses. A builder diffing the rail logic between the two files will find it identical; only the container changed.

Open

Q2 in entities.md asks whether criteria are free text, a checklist, or a structured document. This wireframe draws the checklist answer (a decision, not yet ratified). The raw list now shows the two-signature shape rather than accepted.

What a signature covers is not settled. This screen assumes one signature per line over that line's own text, which is what makes a per-line edit clear only that line. An alternative is one signature over the whole document, which is simpler to verify and loses per-line granularity. The map specifies per line, so that is what is drawn.

Signature display is truncated here to a recognisable prefix. A full multibase signature per line per party would be four lines of base58 on a screen whose subject is readability. The verify page is where a full signature belongs.
