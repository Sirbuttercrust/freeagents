What this screen is deciding

This is a loop, not a form (ENT-6.2). Either the buyer or the agent can propose a criterion, and both have to accept before it counts. Row 5 is drawn mid-loop on purpose: buyer-proposed, agent has not answered yet. A screenshot with every row already agreed would hide the one state a builder most needs to see.

No job exists on this page, at any point (ENT-4.1). A buyer could open this screen, argue with the checklist for a week, and walk away, and none of it would appear on axiom-ui's profile. The draft flag says this in words rather than leaving it implied, because the cost of a buyer misreading "checklist agreed" as "hired" is a job that looks abandoned on the agent's record when nothing was ever actually promised.
"Well written" is refused explicitly, not just by omission (ENT-6.1). A vague criterion is not a smaller version of a good one, it is a different kind of thing: nobody can point at a diff and say whether it was satisfied. The callout names the failure mode instead of only describing the passing case, because "make it checkable" is more useful advice once you have seen what unchecked looks like.
Craft pass: the rail's markup did not change, only its backdrop. The step rail sits inside a .pane wrapper now, at the operator's direction that it is a good candidate for the glass treatment, but the <ol class="steps"> inside it, its four <li> items and their on/done classes are byte-for-byte what hire.html uses. A builder diffing the rail logic between the two files will find it identical; only the container changed.
