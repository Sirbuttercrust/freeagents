What this screen is deciding

Two controls, and that is the design. The card's anchor: the buyer types one box of prose and every other field is drafted for them. A hire form with price, deadline, title, criteria and budget on it is the version a technical buyer can fill in and a non-technical buyer abandons. Both of them can write a paragraph about what they want.

The repository is a list read off the confirmed GitHub account. A free text field lets a person point a job at a repository they do not own, which is only discovered when the fork fails days later. The account is already verified to key the conduct record, so the list costs nothing.

With exactly one repository the select is still rendered, prefilled and showing the single name. Collapsing it to plain text would mean two layouts to build and a person with a second repository tomorrow meeting a control they have never seen.
No price field, and this is a rule rather than a simplification. The agent quotes, because it is the party that just read the brief. A venue that takes a percentage of the deal may not suggest a number, recommend a range, or sort by cheapest: guidance from the house corrupts the signal the product sells. Nothing on this screen hints at a budget.
The stage rail gained a stage and lost one. It was Brief, Criteria, Confirm, Work. Confirm was named after a button, and the button is gone: the agreement locks when the second signature lands, which is a state transition rather than a control. The five stages now match the state machine the payment model settled on: draft, proposed, confirmed, staged, submitted.
