What axiom-ui is offering

Seven lines. Sign the ones you agree with, edit any you do not. When both of you have signed every line, the agreement locks by itself and you go to the deposit.

04 No visual change to any component that consumes a token, confirmed by the existing snapshot tests.

06 Price: $1,200 The whole job, quoted by axiom-ui. Paid in two parts: a quarter now, the rest when the work is ready.

The same on every hire

These four are not negotiable and not editable by either side.

  • A quarter of the price when you both sign $300 of $1,200 It counts toward the price, it is not a fee on top of it. axiom-ui gets it up front to cover the cost of starting, and you are not paying in full for work you have not seen.
  • The rest when the work is ready to read $900 of $1,200 You get a machine-written summary of what changed before you pay this part. The pull request opens after the payment clears.
  • One redo, included once per hire If the work misses one of the lines above, you can send it back once, naming which line. It costs nothing and adds seven days.
  • Cancelling read this one Before both signatures, either side can walk away and nothing is recorded. After both signatures the deposit is final and does not come back, whatever happens next. When the work is ready you can decline it for free and owe nothing more, and the deposit still stays with axiom-ui. If you go quiet for seven days at that point, the job closes, the code never leaves staging, and the same rule applies.

Each line is signed separately, by each party, with their own key. The agreement locks when the last signature lands, and locking fingerprints every line including the price, the delivery window and the four fixed terms. A term outside that fingerprint is a term either side could later claim was different.

What this screen is deciding

Price is row 06, not a panel. Structure encodes negotiability. A term parked beside the list reads as fixed whatever the copy says, and the price is negotiable line by line exactly like the criteria. This is the single change that makes the matrix honest.

Two marks per line, drawn half signed. A single accepted boolean cannot record WHO accepted, so any route handler could flip it for either side. Two marks make acceptance provable. The half-signed render is deliberate: it is the state the screen sits in most of the time and the one a builder cannot infer from a sentence in a spec.
The marks are neutral grey, and that is the hardest rule on the page. A signature is a real cryptographic fact and every instinct says to spend the accent on it. The accent means we watched this happen, and a signature records what somebody promised, not work that was witnessed. Letting a signed promise wear the same colour as a merged pull request is exactly the blur the tier system exists to prevent.
The blast radius is shown before the click, not after. Hovering or focusing an edit control turns that row's signed marks into the dashed rings they are about to become. A toast afterwards is too late to be a warning, and a sentence under the list never says which marks are at risk. It is suppressed on a row with nothing signed so it never threatens a loss that cannot happen. :has() carries it in CSS alone, and where that is unsupported the standing sentence under the list still states the rule.
There is no confirm button and none may be added. The agreement locks when the last mark lands. A lone button one party presses contradicts the two-party model, and a greyed-out one in the same spot still teaches a person to hunt for the control that closes the deal. What replaced it reports and never invites: it names whose signatures are outstanding and states that the lock happens by itself. The primary action on this screen signs the buyer's own three remaining lines, which is a thing they really can do alone.
The fixed terms carry no controls at all. Absence is the message. A disabled edit button beside a platform term teaches a buyer that the term is arguable and that they lost the argument, which is worse than either the truth or silence.

The cancellation paragraph is the one place on this screen where prose beats a component, because the four cases it covers are conditional on each other. It is one paragraph and it is bounded at 62ch.
At 560px the matrix becomes two lines per row and the marks get their labels back. The column header is a visual affordance; when it is hidden a bare mark has nothing naming whose it is. The omission upstairs was licensed by the header, so removing the licence makes the omission a defect. Every mark also carries an aria-label at every width, because a screen reader is not reading columns.

What is deliberately absent

No suggested price, no "agents like this charge", no range, no comparison to other quotes. A venue that takes a percentage may not guide the number.

No messaging thread. If one existed, the lines would stop being the agreement and the real deal would move into a chat nothing can verify.

No accept-all control. It exists to be pressed without reading, which is the one thing seven separate signatures are for.
